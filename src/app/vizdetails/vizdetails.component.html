Top bars: disease terms per gene or combination of genes

Each column is one gene or a combination of two to four genes (the filled dots). Its height is the number of shared disease terms: HPO terms that every gene of the column is annotated with, directly or through a more specific term, and that at least one of those genes is annotated with explicitly. When the genes use terms at different levels (A has “Focal seizure”, B has “Seizure”), the more general term stands for the shared annotation and is counted once. Ancestors that no gene annotates are not counted.

The green part is how many of the shared terms the individual matches: the term is one of the individual’s observed terms or an ancestor of one. A gene annotated with “Seizure” matches an individual with “Focal seizure”, but not the other way around. Each shared term is counted once, however many observed terms fall under it.

Combinations are inclusive: the A+B column also counts terms that a third gene has. Single-gene columns are always shown. A combination is shown only if each of its genes matches at least one observed term and the combination itself has a match. Columns are ordered by the green count, then by height.

These bars count disease terms, whereas the left bars count the individual’s terms, so the numbers can differ: several observed terms may fall under a single disease term.

Segments with no terms are not drawn but are listed here. With more than four genes, only the combinations that occur are listed.
